# THE TOWER — East/West Floor Bible
## Levels 40–131 | Working architectural authority

**Source priority:** The attached *The Tower — Reconciled Working Draft* is the primary canon source for this document. A placement marked **CANON** is explicit in that draft. **STRONG INFERENCE** follows directly from explicit geography, sequence, or system behavior. **AUTHOR LOCK** is an explicit architectural decision now authoritative for this bible, even when the prose has not yet stated the number. **PROPOSED** is new architecture added to make the known system physically and socially coherent but not yet fixed. **RESERVED** means intentionally undeveloped: structurally plausible space is acknowledged, but no named department, scene function, or detailed invention is locked there. **UNRESOLVED** means the draft does not provide enough evidence, or two pieces of evidence cannot yet be reconciled.

This bible maps only Levels 40–131. Below Level 40 is **UNKNOWN / BROOD TERRITORY**. Level 132 and everything beyond it is **UNKNOWN BEYOND MIRROR WALL**. Neither mystery is explained here.

## AUTHOR LOCK — explicit floor architecture

The following placements are authoritative for the current Tower bible:

- **131:** Enclosed Observation Deck; final known boundary. Level 132 and beyond remain **UNKNOWN BEYOND MIRROR WALL**.
- **130–126:** Penthouse suite living quarters, the inhabited Crown.
- **125–123:** Filtration and its service crown; Filtration proper is Level 123.
- **122–121:** Sail Rigging in both towers, with the same-level rope, tension-line, guide, and deployment systems crossing the central gap. Each rigging section has compact crew quarters.
- **121:** Primarily Rigging. The West-side Copperfield residential threshold and hidden-panel entry exist here, but this is not the main general residential floor.
- **120:** Main Living Quarters / Water Maintainers level. Houdini’s lower sleeping quarter and the route toward the central spine are here; the level also has a local infirmary/birthing room.
- **119:** Central Spine / worker crossing and upper-sector service transition.
- **Stair circulation:** Each tower has its own central staircase/core. These are independent vertical routes within the West and East towers; they are supplemented by the shared central elevator banks, service shafts, ladders, ducts, and controlled cross-Tower passages.
- **118–113:** Water Storage Node A, a multi-level tank/warehouse complex with Water Maintainer quarters and local gravity-feed infrastructure.
- **112–106:** Agro Node A, a multi-level production complex; Level 108 contains the broken Agro A skybridge condition.
- **105–103:** Distribution Hub A, including local wash, sorting, transfer, and Medical Substation A at Level 104.
- **100, 70, 50:** Sector infirmaries serving populations that cannot routinely travel to the main Medical band.
- **93–88 / 71–66:** Water Storage Nodes B and C; distributed redundancy rather than one central reservoir.
- **87–82 / 65–60:** Agro Nodes B and C; distributed food production and maintenance.
- **81–79 / 59–57:** Distribution Hubs B and C, with Surgical/Trauma at 80 and Medical Substations B/C at 79/58.
- **48:** East elevator sister substation beside Records and Storage; pharmacy/medical stores occupy the controlled approach.
- **47:** West elevator substation, two levels above the top Medical level.
- **46:** Guardian Medical on the opposite tower, reached after the Level 45 crossing and service-level rise.
- **45–43:** Main Medical band; Level 45 is Medical Intake, with treatment, triage, surgery support, and service distributed through the three levels.
- **40:** Lowest known normal-elevator landing and lower convergence. Anything below it is **UNKNOWN / BROOD TERRITORY**.
- **Transportation:** Two elevator banks, four cars total, run in the central system between the towers. Stairs, service elevators, shafts, ladders, ducts, risers, and controlled skybridges provide redundancy.

These locks govern the master map, the visual schematic, and future interactive routes. A lock can still be revised by the author, but it must not be silently contradicted by a later floor assignment.

### Reading the master map

The repeated residential/service band from roughly 120 to 41 is intentional but provisional. The prose proves the Tower is socially stratified and that living quarters, water work, Medical, Agro, Distribution, Sailors, Filtration, and Records are connected by routes. It does not prove that every department occupies a unique floor. A real Tower would repeat residences, utility chases, pipe risers, ventilation, storage, inspection galleries, and repair access across many levels. This map therefore gives future stories a physically plausible default without pretending that every generic cell is a locked scene location.

## 3. Functional zones

### Zone A — Upper known boundary and service crown: Levels 131–123

**Working function:** Level 131 is the upper known boundary; Levels 130–126 are penthouse suite living quarters; Levels 125–123 are the filtration/service crown.

**Canon basis:** Filtration is in the highest service levels; Brooks’s gauge ritual and the cascade failure are central; Jodie leaves from the lowest Filtration level by duct. The penthouse placement is an author-directed working lock.

**Architecture:** Keep Levels 130–126 socially insulated and deliberately finished: private suites, controlled lifts, staff/service circulation, and restricted access toward the machinery below. Keep Levels 125–123 machinery-heavy, with noisy process rooms, narrow catwalks, gauge niches, dampers, filter housings, chemical/dirty-side separation, worker shortcuts, and compact attached filtration-crew quarters. These quarters contain bunks, wash points, emergency food/air, tool lockers, and watch stations. The service crown needs emergency bypasses, because a single failed floor cannot be allowed to poison the whole air system.

**Open placement:** Whether Filtration is West-only, split East/West, or West-primary with East redundancy remains unresolved. The prose later says “West Filtration,” making West a canon Filtration destination, but it does not exclude East filtration support.

### Zone B — Continuous Sail Rigging: Levels 122–121

**Working function:** Sail deployment, pulleys, tension lines, winches, guides, counterweights, and continuous rope/tension transfer through both towers and the central gap.

**Canon basis:** Jodie drops from Filtration to the highest rigging section, then one level lower; “ropes of life” are the Sailors’ systems.

**Architecture:** Rigging is a paired same-level system: West Rigging and East Rigging occupy Levels 122–121, while the central rigging spine carries ropes and tension lines between them. Each rigging section has a small attached living quarter for sailors/rigging crews—bunks, wash point, food locker, emergency air, and a door into service circulation—but not a general residential population. The system should occupy tall, vertically continuous volumes. A level can have a normal deck but still be pierced by ropes, lines, counterweights, and inspection ladders. Some rigging passes through floors, walls, and the gap between towers. These are ideal early interactive spaces because a character can stop at a catwalk, take a maintenance ladder, or enter a service hatch without contradicting the main route.

### Zone C — Distributed Residential / Worker Support Network

**Working function:** Residential life, water-maintainer homes, pipe service, communal rooms, small workshops, laundry support, and local storage.

**Canon basis:** Living Quarters are populated and socially legible; Water Maintainers occupy one route level; Jodie is recognized as an outsider; the Tower’s people are assigned to sections and shifts.

**Architecture:** This zone should be mixed-use, not a residential hotel. Every residential cluster needs water risers, waste/ventilation chases, food preparation, storage alcoves, repair closets, and quiet bypasses. The West side can hold denser general population and worker housing; the East side can hold more Water Maintainer housing and service access, but that East/West split is proposed, not canon.

### Zone D — Water Storage: distributed multi-level nodes

**Working function:** Large tanks, pressure valves, purge cycles, gauges, drains, service passages, and gravity-feed distribution.

**Canon basis:** Water Storage is on the mechanical side reached after the central spine; Tank Four/Tank Five and the twelve-second purge window are explicit; sailors capture moisture through upper rigging and store it in tanks above inhabited levels; gravity feeds patched lines.

**Architecture:** Water Storage is distributed into at least three multi-level nodes: an upper node around Levels 118–113 used by Jodie, a middle node around 93–88, and a lower node around 71–66. Each node includes tank galleries, reserve warehouses, pressure-control decks, drains, isolation valves, pipe risers, local gravity-feed dispatch, and compact attached Water Maintainer quarters. These quarters contain bunks, a wash point, food storage, emergency air, tool lockers, and direct service access; they are work housing, not general residential neighborhoods. This prevents one rupture, contamination event, or blocked route from eliminating the Tower’s water supply.

### Zone E — Sailors’ service and distribution route: exact band unresolved

**Working function:** Moisture capture, rigging maintenance, transfer of water and supplies, pipe service, and old central-route access.

**Canon basis:** The official escort route crosses Sailors’ levels; Sail Rigging belongs to sailors; water is caught through upper rigging.

**Architecture:** Sailors should be distributed between upper rigging and lower water systems. This creates a believable reason for them to know both sides of the Tower and for the official route to cross their levels.

### Zone F — Agro: distributed multi-level grow systems

**Working function:** Vertical food production, grow lights, trays, hanging lines, nutrient concentrates, mold/pest treatment, raw-material transfer.

**Canon basis:** Jodie enters Agro from Water Storage; the space is approximately twenty feet high; Tyler is an Agro worker; fertilizer exposure causes Medical cases; a raw-material platform descends to Distribution.

**Architecture:** Agro is not one department on one floor. It has at least three multi-level production nodes: an upper node around Levels 112–106, a middle node around 87–82, and a lower node around 65–60. Each node contains tall grow bays, wet floors, drains, nutrient lines, grow-light trusses, raw-material platforms, quarantine/cleanup routes, and maintenance decks. Water and Agro nodes are paired locally but not perfectly one-to-one; the Tower can route water around a failed agricultural node.

### Zone G — Distribution: exact band unresolved

**Working function:** Receiving, processing, quota control, rinse stations, jumpsuit issue, industrial laundry/wash, conveyor and transfer interfaces.

**Canon basis:** Honey arrives from Agro; Distribution rinses Jodie, processes the clothing, uses quota boards, and links into the older central passage.

**Architecture:** Distribution follows each major Agro node in local hubs: upper Hub A around Levels 105–103, middle Hub B around 81–79, and lower Hub C around 59–57. These are not identical; one may specialize in food, another in parts/waste, another in clothing, rinsing, and quota processing. Redundant conveyors and floor-level transfer lanes keep the system alive when a hub is blocked. Workers know exactly what paperwork a mistake would trigger and therefore quietly repair it.

### Zone G.1 — Other systems the Tower must contain

These are necessary infrastructure, not extra themed floors. They may occupy partial levels, shared machinery decks, or repeating service rooms:

- **Power generation and substations:** paired elevator power, local distribution, emergency batteries, relay rooms, and isolated machine spaces.
- **Waste, sewage, and reclamation:** gravity waste lines, composting/biological treatment, salvage sorting, and hazardous-material isolation.
- **Air, ventilation, and pressure control:** ducts, dampers, scrubbers, fan rooms, condensation drains, and dead/abandoned passages.
- **Fabrication and repair:** machine shops, welding/metal work, seal and gasket stores, tool libraries, rigging repair, and improvised parts production.
- **Laundry, sanitation, and quarantine:** industrial wash, clean/dirty corridors, pest control, isolation rooms, and clothing issue.
- **Medical support:** pharmacy/material stores, oxygen/air supplies, morgue or body-handling space, clean instruments, and emergency overflow.
- **Records, education, and communications:** records vaults, dispatch rooms, hand-copying/teaching areas, message chains, public notices, and route maps.
- **Security and fire response:** Guardian posts, checkpoints, fire suppression, rescue equipment, controlled doors, and evacuation staging.
- **Residential support:** kitchens, communal eating, child care, sleeping overflow, recreation, workshops, and social spaces.

The map should show these as recurring service/support conditions, not as ninety-two unique departments.

### Zone G.3 — Fire suppression, power, and dead waterworks

#### Fire suppression

The Tower cannot rely on a functioning automatic sprinkler grid. Fire protection is a distributed labor system built around whatever remains usable:

- **Fire crews at every major node:** Agro, Distribution, Water Storage, Filtration, Medical, and residential sectors each maintain trained response teams.
- **Dry risers and manual hose points:** many old pipes remain in walls, but some are dry, cracked, or disconnected from pressure. Crews must know which lines can still be trusted.
- **Local suppression stores:** water drums, foam, sand, blankets, masks, cutting tools, breathing gear, and firebreak materials sit near work populations.
- **Firebreak doors and shutters:** especially around Agro grow bays, filtration machinery, power rooms, elevator shafts, and medical clean-air areas.
- **Named crews:** a local fire team may be called **Ash Hands**, **Red Watch**, or simply **the Bucket Line**, depending on the sector and its history.

Fire suppression is one reason small living quarters and first-aid rooms sit beside dangerous departments. Workers do not have time to travel six floors for breathing gear while a grow-light rig or pressure room burns.

#### Power stations

The original Tower-wide power system has failed in sections, so power is generated, stored, switched, and rationed locally:

- **Upper service power:** relay rooms and emergency batteries near Filtration and the penthouse/service boundary.
- **Agro power rooms:** local generators, lighting banks, battery racks, and heat-management equipment attached to each Agro node.
- **Distribution power rooms:** motor controls for conveyors, wash units, lifts, and quota-processing equipment.
- **Medical power:** isolated circuits for surgery, clean air, refrigeration, oxygen/air equipment, and emergency lighting.
- **Lower paired elevator substations:** West Level 47 and East Level 48 feed the two central elevator banks, four cars total.
- **Emergency power:** hand-started generators, flywheels, scavenged batteries, and priority circuits that can keep only the most essential systems alive.

Power stations should feel like inhabited danger zones: hot rooms, relay sounds, locked switchgear, improvised labels, battery smell, and crews who know which lights can be sacrificed to keep a surgical room or water pump alive.

#### Disabled and abandoned water facilities

The Tower still has water, but much of the original water infrastructure is dead. The old central water mains, pumping rooms, purification chambers, pressure regulators, and automatic distribution systems stopped working decades earlier. Their pipes remain as silent arteries through the walls and central spine.

The active system is newer and more improvised: sailors capture moisture through upper rigging, Water Maintainers collect and store it in distributed tank nodes, and gravity feeds patched lines where possible. Some active lines run beside dead lines for entire levels. A valve may have a formal label nobody trusts and a private mark that tells workers which one actually moves water.

Dead water facilities create exploration spaces without solving the lower mystery: dry pump rooms, empty reservoirs, corroded filter halls, abandoned pressure chambers, sealed maintenance wells, and pipe galleries where condensation appears even though the official system is inactive. They are **ABANDONED / DISABLED**, not secret functioning infrastructure unless a future story explicitly reactivates one.

### Zone H — Medical / Guardian Medical: Levels 45–43 and Level 46 working lock

**Working function:** Medical Intake, waiting, Triage, treatment, clean support, Guardian Medical, service junctions, and cross-Tower passage.

**Canon basis:** Medical Intake is above Level 40; Jodie exits at Medical Intake; Guardian Medical cuts across the divide; Hollywood, Tyler, and Clyde operate in this band. **Working placement:** Medical 45–43; Guardian Medical 46.

**Architecture:** Medical needs a public intake face, controlled treatment space, service/laundry back routes, isolation, clean/dirty circulation, supplies, and a Guardian-controlled cross-Tower passage. It should be one of the most navigable zones because life-safety requires signs and access—even if bureaucracy controls who may use them.

### Zone H.1 — Distributed Medical Network

The Tower’s medical system is a network, not one hospital. Because residents are assigned to sectors, cannot move freely, and lived for decades without reliable elevators, essential care must be available close to where people live and work:

- **Sector infirmaries:** small but permanent clinics embedded in the residential bands around Levels 120, 100, 70, and 50. Each has treatment beds, a medic/nurse station, basic pharmacy stock, wash/sanitation, splints, oxygen or emergency air, childbirth supplies, and a way to isolate contagious or chemically exposed patients.
- **First-aid posts:** smaller treatment rooms attached to every Agro and Distribution node, with splints, wash stations, exposure kits, oxygen, and a stretcher point. These are not the same thing as a full clinic.
- **Medical Substation A, Level 104:** upper-node exposure treatment and stabilization for Agro A, Distribution A, and nearby water/service crews.
- **Surgical / Trauma Center, Level 80:** operating rooms, recovery, sterile support, surgical stores, and controlled patient movement.
- **Medical Substation B, Level 79:** surgical intake and trauma stabilization connected directly to the mid-Tower work population.
- **Medical Substation C, Level 58:** quarantine, isolation, exposure observation, and clean/dirty separation for the lower production nodes.
- **Pharmacy and medical stores, Level 48:** controlled supplies near Records and Storage and the East elevator substation.
- **Main Medical, Levels 45–43:** Intake, Triage, general treatment, Medical support, and the primary lower medical hub.
- **Guardian Medical, Level 46:** controlled medical/security passage and cross-Tower routing.

This structure lets a worker receive immediate care locally, while serious cases move by stretcher, service route, or elevator to surgery or Main Medical. It also creates practical reasons for medical personnel, supplies, patients, and information to move through the Tower. The fact that the elevators later return is a revolution precisely because the Tower’s distributed medical network had been compensating for their absence for generations.

### Zone I — Records and Storage: East side, centered at Level 48

**Working function:** Records, parts, gaskets, stores, old documentation, controlled issue, and likely associated receiving/storage.

**Canon basis:** The gasket is here; it is on the East side, several dozen levels from Filtration; Guardian Medical reaches the opposite side a few levels below it; Jodie follows an official route upward. **Working placement:** Records and Storage is adjacent to the East elevator substation at Level 48.

Records needs dry, secure, stable rooms; mechanical storage needs bins, shelves, spares, and issue counters; the two can share a controlled department. The East sister elevator substation is AUTHOR LOCK at Level 48, immediately adjacent to the Records/Storage approach.

**Canon basis:** Two lower substations must be activated together; Jodie goes East toward Records while Eli goes West toward Medical; the recovered car returns to 40; attack routes converge at 40.

The central system can descend below 40, but every such route is labeled UNKNOWN / BROOD TERRITORY and is not developed here.

## 4. Transportation map

### Normal elevators

- **Known service range:** Levels 40–131 are the normal known range for this bible. The prose explicitly establishes Level 40 as a returned landing and the known upper boundary at 131, but it does not state whether every car stops at every floor.
- **Elevator geometry:** There are **two elevator banks, four cars total**, located in the central transportation core between the East and West towers. Each bank contains two parallel cars/shafts. The banks are distinct enough to permit redundancy, maintenance isolation, and different stop patterns.
- **Working rule:** Main passenger cars stop at designated occupied/service landings; service cars and emergency modes bypass ordinary stops. This is **PROPOSED** and exists to preserve the prose’s elevator descent past multiple floors without requiring those floors to be empty.
- **Upper cars:** The draft says the elevator cars are “somewhere above” when the lower system is restored. This now means the four central cars and/or their upper machine/control spaces are distributed through the central core above the restoration zone. Their exact machine rooms and storage levels remain **UNRESOLVED**. Do not assign them to 131 or the mirror wall without author decision.
- **Lower interface:** A car descends past the restoration team toward below 40 before emergency brakes catch it. The shaft may continue below 40, but the destination is **UNKNOWN / BROOD TERRITORY**.
- **Substations:** Two lower substations, East and West, are **CANON** as paired control/power nodes. Their working placements are now **West Level 47** and **East Level 48**. Both feed the central elevator banks rather than separate tower-side elevator shafts.
- **Sister substation near Records:** The East substation at Level 48 is next to Records and Storage. Guardian Medical is at Level 46, making Level 48 two levels above it. This is AUTHOR LOCK for the current architecture; the underlying prose relationship remains separately labeled CANON or inference where appropriate.

### Service elevators and bypasses

The four central cars should have different stop profiles: ordinary cars serve populated hubs; service cars stop at machinery decks, tank galleries, Agro transfer levels, Distribution, Medical service, Records receiving, and substation levels; emergency mode bypasses ordinary stops. These are **PROPOSED**. They explain how heavy tanks, food, parts, waste, and medical supplies move without sending every load through residential corridors. Some landing doors can be dead, sealed, or manually operated even when the shaft continues.

### Stairs

Each tower has its own central staircase/core. These are the primary long-distance routes inside the West and East towers after the elevator fails. The prose proves that stairs connect Medical-side and Guardian routes, that the attack uses stairs, that evacuation proceeds through stairs, and that at least one service stair descends two levels to the transfer crawlspace. The two central staircases should therefore be continuous but segmented by checkpoints, welded doors, pressure doors, and local jurisdiction. Secondary fire stairs, ladders, and maintenance cuts may branch from them, but their number and condition remain open.

### Ladders and shafts

Ladders serve elevator shafts, rigging, ducts, tank galleries, and old service access. The Filtration-to-Rigging duct and the Distribution-to-Medical elevator shaft are **CANON**. Person-sized hidden shafts behind removable panels are **CANON** in the living route. Their exact map coordinates are not all known.

### Transfer belts

The old belt system is a genuine cross-Tower logistics network:

1. **First belt:** dead. **CANON.**
2. **Second belt:** moves unpredictably in the wrong direction and goes toward below; Jodie avoids it. Its exact destination must remain **UNKNOWN / BROOD TERRITORY** if it continues below 40. **CANON / UNRESOLVED.**
3. **Third belt:** mostly works and crosses toward the East side. It makes three knocks near the exit and requires the rider to leave before the last roller. **CANON.**

The belts originally carried food, parts, waste, and miscellaneous supplies. Their presence justifies a central belt gallery with maintenance ledges, access hatches, guardrails, and abandoned branches. The exact floor of the belt gallery is unresolved; do not force it into a specific number until Medical’s absolute level is locked.

### Central-spine crossings

Crossings should be rare enough to matter and ordinary enough to be maintained:

- **Rigging/living hidden panel:** worker route; **CANON**.
- **Water Maintainer crossing:** suit/mask-mediated route through the central spine; **CANON**.
- **Guardian Medical passage:** controlled cross-Tower route; **CANON**.
- **Old transfer belts:** supply route; **CANON**.
- **Official stair-and-corridor route:** Guardian-controlled route through Medical, Distribution, Agro, Water, and Sailors’ levels; **CANON**.
- **Abandoned transfer bridges:** may exist at other levels as **PROPOSED**, but each requires a structural or logistics reason and should not be added merely to provide shortcuts.

These are not interchangeable with every central-spine passage: a skybridge is a major structural/logistics connection with its own doors, pressure seals, inspection access, and failure history.
